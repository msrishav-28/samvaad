# Samvaad

**Practice the moment before you speak.**

Developed for **Gnani AI's The Great Indian AI Internship Challenge**.

## The need

Students can prepare an answer and still struggle with the next question. Interviews, vivas, debates, group discussions and presentations demand clear reasoning, relevant examples and recovery under challenge. A consistent practice partner and specific feedback can make deliberate rehearsal more accessible.

## The solution

Samvaad turns speaking preparation into a repeatable loop: **speak, respond, reflect and practise again.** Its current implementation offers five English practice formats, curated topics, three- or five-minute rounds, and Gentle, Standard or Intense pressure. Group discussion uses an AI moderator and two AI peers; other formats use one AI counterpart. Presentation practice protects delivery time before questions. Participants are disclosed as AI.

## Feedback students can use

After a successfully completed, verified conversation, students receive eight dimension scores with reasons, supporting quotes where available, strongest and weakest moments, and exactly three practice actions. The application checks quotes against the transcript and calculates the overall score. History supports review and comparison of compatible attempts. Feedback evaluates recognized words, not pronunciation, emotion or vocal confidence; it remains AI coaching, not an exam or hiring verdict.

## Built today

The student journey includes accounts, setup, a microphone check, voice controls, processing, reports and repeat practice. Gnani Prisma recognizes speech; Gnani Timbre voices the AI participants. Groq-hosted GPT-OSS 120B is the selected reasoning model for replies and scoring. Supabase provides authentication and PostgreSQL; Next.js serves the interface. Samvaad stores transcripts and feedback without storing raw audio.

## Next steps toward the vision

The vision is a credible, supportive but demanding rehearsal partner. The next priorities are dependable session completion; role and experience briefs for interviews; trusted subject material for vivas; audience and objective briefs for presentations; and clearer debate phases and fairer group turn-taking. Repeat sessions could actively target earlier practice actions. Real-student trials must establish whether the exercises feel relevant and the feedback helps students make useful changes. These are development directions, not delivered capabilities or proven outcomes.

## Current stage

Complete synthetic voice-to-report rounds have been demonstrated, but intermittent voice-upload failures remain unresolved. Consistent live completion, final-word accuracy, physical devices, provider privacy terms, operating cost and student acceptance still need verification. The intended benefit is confidence through better preparation and repetition; improvement is not yet proven or guaranteed.
